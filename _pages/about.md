I am **Dandan (Alina) Tan (谭单单)**, a Ph.D. candidate in Quantitative Life Sciences at [McGill University](https://www.mcgill.ca/), co-supervised by **Dr. Guillaume Butler-Laporte** and **Dr. Brent Richards** at the Lady Davis Institute.

I study how human genetic variation shapes disease susceptibility and informs therapeutic discovery. My work combines **statistical genetics, genetic epidemiology, and proteomics** to connect population-scale associations with biological mechanisms.

My doctoral research focuses on the genetics of infectious diseases, including dermatophytosis and sepsis. I integrate evidence from large biobanks—including UK Biobank, the Million Veteran Program, FinnGen, and All of Us—using GWAS meta-analysis, Mendelian randomization, and colocalization. I am also interested in mobile element variation and the molecular heterogeneity of complex diseases.

Before starting my Ph.D. in 2023, I completed a B.Sc. at McGill University, majoring in Pharmacology with a minor in Computer Science and graduating with Distinction. My doctoral work is supported by an FRQS Doctoral Training Award and a MUHC Metabolic Centre of Excellence Fellowship.

## Research interests

- **Human genetics:** disease susceptibility across populations and biobanks.
- **Causal inference:** connecting genes and circulating proteins to disease risk.
- **Translational genomics:** prioritizing potential therapeutic targets.
- **Complex disease heterogeneity:** integrating molecular profiles with clinical variation.
